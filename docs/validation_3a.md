# Phase 3a — Inviscid CFD validation, M = 3, 2-ramp design

## Setup

- Ansys Fluent 2026 R1 Student, 2D planar, density-based, implicit, Roe-FDS flux scheme,
  second-order upwind spatial discretization.
- Inviscid, ideal-gas air (gamma = 1.4), operating pressure 0 Pa.
- Freestream: p = 10 000 Pa, T = 220 K, M = 3. Applied via pressure-far-field on the inlet and
  top (`TOP_FREESTREAM`) boundaries.
- Outlet: pressure-outlet (supersonic, extrapolated).
- Domain: the design-point domain generated by `inletdesign cfd-domain`, capture height 0.1 m.
- Mesh: 118 911 cells (216 tri, remainder quad), nominal size 0.5 mm, minimum orthogonal
  quality 0.207.
- Convergence: ~2980 iterations, residuals ~3e-6, net mass imbalance -5.9e-7 kg/s
  (~4e-8 relative).

## Discussion

The shocks are smeared over roughly 3-4 cells at this mesh density (see
`fig_3a_mach_lip_mesh.png`), which is the expected numerical diffusion of a shock-capturing
scheme and accounts for the slightly lower CFD total pressure at the outlet (0.35% below the
closed-form tool value): smeared shocks dissipate a small amount of total pressure beyond the
ideal, infinitesimally-thin-shock assumption built into the tool's compressible-flow relations.

A one-cell-wide low-Mach layer is visible along the cowl wall near the lip corner. This is a
numerical artefact of the lip singularity in an inviscid solution (no boundary layer is modeled),
not a physical flow feature, and is consistent with the corner/leading-edge mesh resolution
rather than any real separation or recirculation.
